1. New structural hypothesis

Consecutive complete ASCII/CRLF records at the bottom of a scroll region could
replace per-line rotations with one bulk rotation. This is materially distinct
from the scanner/decoder, affinity and reader-buffer changes already tried.
Candidate accepted at most eight complete records and at most region-height
minus one; refused pictures/unknown picture motion, nonzero column, insert
mode, non-default charset/template extras and wrap-pending cursor. The existing
print_run path wrote every character. Keeping the first target row preserved
its unwritten suffix. Parser mirror was advanced only over complete ASCII/CRLF.
Nothing deferred across a call. All candidate sources and patches retained.

3. Causal ablation: the truecolor direction does not require CRLF batching

Escape-heavy truecolor/cursor workloads cannot use consecutive ASCII/CRLF
coalescing. V3 also changed inlining, so blaming batching for these gains would
be an attribution error. A second experiment removed ALL batching and changed
only one runtime line: #[inline(never)] on engine::feed. Both arms freshly rebuilt
with the same configured RUSTC 1.99.0, locked dependencies, release thin LTO and
one codegen unit. PATH rustc is 1.99.1 but is not Cargo's configured compiler.
Fresh control exactly matched the prior iteration's fresh-control hash.

Second real-terminal gate: 18 rounds/54 sessions, all six orders repeated three
times. Candidate/fresh-control paired throughput:

metric          median ratio    wins/18    uncorrected two-sided sign p
ASCII              .91049          5              .143463
Unicode           1.00745         12              .237885
truecolor         1.08351         17              .000144958
cursor_motion     1.01457         15              .00753784
scroll_region      .98010          8              .814529
TUI                .99435          6              .237885

This reproduces the truecolor direction without batching. nm records a distinct
standalone feed symbol in the annotation candidate, absent in control. It does
NOT prove an instruction-cache/register-allocation/specialization mechanism.
The annotation was rejected conservatively: the ASCII direction was concerning,
remaining weak metrics did not establish a useful gain. ASCII's sign test is
not conventionally significant; region is inconclusive, NOT a proved regression.
Do not turn this promotion decision into a theorem of universal performance.
